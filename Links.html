NRES 746

Fall 2026

A short list of the resources most useful for this course. Please let me know if there are other links that you think should be included on this page!

Weekly "Stats Chats" at the UNR library: drop-in help with statistics and R.

The textbook

  • Ecological Models and Data in R (Bolker): book page, with R code, data, and errata. The emdbook R package has the data sets.

Math and probability background

Short, visual introductions to the math this course uses. Good places to start if a topic feels shaky.

  • Essence of Calculus (3Blue1Brown): derivatives, integrals, and the fundamental theorem, with almost no algebra.
  • Essence of Linear Algebra (3Blue1Brown): vectors, matrix multiplication, determinants, inverses, and eigenvalues as geometry.
  • Probability (3Blue1Brown): includes Bayes' theorem, the central limit theorem, and why the normal distribution has a \(\pi\) in it (the Gaussian integral).
  • Seeing Theory: interactive introduction to probability, distributions, inference, and regression.
  • The Distribution Explorer: the common distributions, what stories generate them, and how they relate, with interactive plots.

Mathematical statistics

  • The geometry of statistics (Sam Levey): how linear algebra explains regression, variance, and more; a good follow-up to the linear algebra videos.
  • STAT 414: Introduction to Probability Theory and STAT 415: Introduction to Mathematical Statistics (Penn State): free course notes covering random variables, expectation, distributions, likelihood, and maximum likelihood estimation, with worked examples.
  • Statistical Rethinking (McElreath): book and free lecture videos. A readable, ecology-friendly path through likelihood, Bayesian inference, multilevel models, and causal reasoning.

R and RStudio

  • Download R and RStudio.
  • RStudio projects: keep each analysis in its own folder.
  • R Bootcamp: my introductory R workshop.
  • Hands-On Programming with R (Grolemund): writing your own functions, loops, and simulations.
  • R for Data Science (Wickham et al.): data wrangling and plotting with the tidyverse.
  • Coding Club: short R tutorials for ecologists and environmental scientists.
  • Posit cheatsheets: one- or two-page references for R Markdown, dplyr, ggplot2, and more.
  • R Markdown: The Definitive Guide (Xie et al.): reports, slides, and websites (this site is built with R Markdown).
  • Happy Git and GitHub for the useR (Bryan): version control with Git and GitHub from RStudio.

Bayesian inference, MCMC, and Stan

  • Bayes Rules! (Johnson, Ott, and Dogucu): a free introductory Bayesian textbook with R code.
  • Interactive gallery of MCMC samplers (Chi Feng): watch Metropolis, Gibbs, HMC, and NUTS explore different posteriors.
  • MCMC sampling for dummies (Wiecki): a Metropolis sampler built from scratch.
  • A conceptual introduction to Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (Betancourt): why HMC works, with very little math.
  • Stan: the documentation (user's guide, functions reference) and the getting started with cmdstanr vignette.
  • brms: fit Stan models with lme4-style formulas.
  • Bayesian workflow (Gelman et al.): building, checking, and comparing models in practice.
  • NIMBLE and JAGS: BUGS-language samplers, useful for models with discrete latent variables.

Model comparison and performance

  • A practical guide to selecting models for exploration, inference, and prediction in ecology (Tredennick et al. 2021, Ecology).
  • Cross-validation FAQ (Vehtari): what cross-validation and LOO estimate, and when to trust them. See also the loo package vignettes.
  • An Introduction to Statistical Learning (James et al.): free book; its chapters on cross-validation and on ridge and lasso regression are especially clear.
  • DHARMa: simulation-based residual checks for GLMs and mixed models.

Causal inference

  • DAGitty: draw a causal diagram and find which variables to adjust for.
  • The Effect (Huntington-Klein): a free, readable book on causal diagrams and research design.

Mixed models

  • GLMM FAQ (Bolker): the standard reference for practical questions about fitting mixed models in R (convergence warnings, singular fits, choosing a package, testing random effects).
  • A brief introduction to mixed effects modelling and multi-model inference in ecology (Harrison et al. 2018, PeerJ).
  • Fitting linear mixed-effects models using lme4 (Bates et al. 2015): how lme4 works, including the formula syntax.
  • glmmTMB vignette: zero-inflated and dispersion models.

Smooths and GAMs

  • GAMs in R (Ross): a free interactive course using mgcv.
  • Hierarchical generalized additive models in ecology (Pedersen et al. 2019, PeerJ).

Time series and spatial data

  • Applied time series analysis for fisheries and environmental sciences (Holmes, Scheuerell, and Ward): labs on ARMA and state-space models.
  • Forecasting: Principles and Practice (Hyndman and Athanasopoulos): autocorrelation, ARIMA, and forecasting.
  • Geocomputation with R (Lovelace et al.): spatial data in R with sf and terra.

Data sources

Public data sets for final projects:

  • Dryad: data behind published papers.
  • EDI Data Portal: LTER and other long-term ecological data.
  • DataONE: searches many environmental data repositories at once.
  • NEON data portal: standardized ecological data from sites across the US.
  • GBIF: species occurrence records worldwide.
